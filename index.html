Room 01 — Divine

Devotional & mythological works.

Each canvas begins as an act of research before it becomes an act of painting — the posture of a deity, the colour of a garment, the placement of an ornament, all drawn from tradition and checked against it. Gesso relief is raised by hand, gold leaf is set by hand, and the pigment is layered in oil, slowly, the way it has been in this family since before either of us was born. What leaves the studio carries the faint, honest texture of a surface a brush actually crossed — never flat, never uniform, never quite the same twice.

01 — Origin

Sapar Brothers, before independence.

Bharat Sapar and his brother Vishnu ran Sapar Brother's, a respected firm, until 2003. When the brothers went their separate ways, Bharat began painting under his own name — carrying the same discipline into a smaller, quieter practice that would become Sapar Arts.

02 — Form

Gesso, gold leaf, and oil.

The Tanjore method builds a painting in relief before it is a painting in colour — layered gesso, gold leaf pressed by hand, then pigment worked in slowly over the raised surface until the image stands slightly off the canvas and catches light differently depending on where you stand.

03 — Devotion

A practice, not a product.

Working alongside his children, Bharat moved between oil, acrylic, watercolour, poster colour and sketch — a family workshop rather than a single style, always returning to the devotional subjects at its centre and to the belief that a painting is finished when it is right, not when it is quick.

04 — Memory

Passed hand to hand.

Bharat Sapar passed away in 2012. Since then, Arvind and Vidya have carried the practice forward — the same brushes, the same patience, the same understanding that nothing here is printed, and that a family's name on a painting is a kind of promise.

Room 02 — Portraits & commissions

Custom work, painted to sit for generations.

Ancestor portraits worked from old photographs, custom deity paintings chosen to suit a family's household shrine, and larger commissions for temples and hotels — each one measured, sketched, and painted on site or in the studio, then finished the same way every Sapar Arts piece is finished: entirely by hand. Every commission begins with a conversation — about the space it will hang in, the story it should carry, and the colours that belong to that particular household or hall — before a single line is drawn.

Room 03 — Culture, kept whole

No prints leave this studio.

Every work carries the same hand that raised the gesso and set the gold — nothing here is reproduced, scanned, or mass-made. What you see is the surface a brush actually touched, held by whoever in the family painted it that season, with the small variations and quiet decisions that only a human hand makes along the way.

Bharat Sapar worked across oil, acrylic, watercolour, poster colour and sketch throughout his life, alongside his children Vidya and Arvind. He passed away in 2012; the studio, and the standard, continue under their hands — unhurried, and unchanged in what it refuses to compromise on.

Founder

Bharat Sapar

Sapar Brother's, until 2003 · Sapar Arts, 2003–2012

Trained in the family firm Sapar Brother's alongside his brother Vishnu, Bharat set out independently in 2003 and spent the following decade painting devotional works with Arvind and Vidya at his side, treating every commission — however small — as worth doing properly.

Second Generation

Arvind Sapar

Painter, Sapar Arts

Continues the studio's devotional and mythological practice, overseeing commissions and the technical craft of gesso relief and gold leaf work passed down from Bharat — the steady hand behind the studio's larger temple and hotel projects.

Second Generation
